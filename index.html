Born in the web.

The next AI infrastructure battle is not about GPUs. It is about the interface between agents and the web — who gets to search it, extract from it, charge for it, and learn from it. WebNative.ai is the name for the side that belongs there.

The shift

Search stopped being a destination.

It became part of the task.

Instead of handing a person links, an agent now searches repeatedly — reads, compares sources, fills gaps — until it can complete the assignment. Google calls the mechanism query fan-out: one question decomposed into sub-questions, run in parallel, synthesized into one answer.

1st

Search is now the agent's first move, not the human's last resort. The retrieval layer decides what the agent can know — and therefore what it can do.

The layer

Three positions are forming.

Integrated platforms, specialized providers, and the people who charge for the door.

PositionWhoRole
Integrated Google / Gemini Bundle models, indexing, retrieval, and agent capabilities in one stack.
Specialized Tavily, Firecrawl, Exa Supply search and extraction across model platforms — infrastructure you can swap the model underneath.
Access & monetization Cloudflare Help content owners control and price automated access: allow, charge, or block — per crawler.

The specialist layer is already consolidating. In February 2026, Nebius — the AI cloud company — announced its acquisition of Tavily, the search API most agent builders reach for first. The agreement was announced February 10 and closed February 19, at a reported $275 million. Tavily keeps its brand and its founder; it now sits inside Nebius's cloud platform. Tavily reports 3 million-plus developers building with it and 100 million-plus requests a month.

On the other side of the door, Cloudflare launched Pay Per Crawl in private beta on July 1, 2025, built on the web's long-dormant HTTP 402 Payment Required response: a publisher sets a price, and each crawler is allowed, charged, or blocked. In July 2026 Cloudflare began evolving it into Pay Per Use — publishers paid when their content actually creates value, not just when it is fetched — and from September 15, 2026, new domains block AI training and agent bots on ad-supported pages by default.

Being online no longer means being freely retrievable. Access is now a permission and a price — which is exactly why the agent that is native to the web, rather than a visitor to it, is the valuable side of the argument.

The trajectory turn

The web became a classroom, not just a library.

The most consequential distinction in the whole argument: an agent's research run — its searches, tool calls, source selections, and results — is a trajectory. Trajectories can be evaluated. The good ones become training data. The web stops being merely text to ingest and becomes an environment for learning how to work.

NVIDIA's account, as reported by Tavily and NVIDIA:

Research runs generated
~80,000
Kept after quality filtering
~67,000
Fine-tuned model
Nemotron 3 Super

Those ~67,000 retained trajectories fine-tuned Nemotron 3 Super in roughly 25 hours on 128 H100 GPUs, and the resulting researcher — NVIDIA AI-Q — reached #1 on DeepResearch Bench I and II. Poor retrieval wastes more than an agent's time and inference budget: it wastes the compute spent generating training examples that get rejected. Better information access improves both application quality and training efficiency.

Figures as reported by Tavily (October 2026) and NVIDIA (Hugging Face). The economics of this layer — market size, margins, durable advantage — are still unproven, and no AI lab has publicly said it pays for crawled access. Read this as a map of an emerging infrastructure layer, not a demonstrated investment case.
